● Conversational AI · Autonomous Systems

Teaching vehicles to understand plain language, not just commands.

ChatPilot is a multi-modal AI agent that lets a driver interact with a simulated autonomous vehicle the way they'd talk to a person — combining voice interaction, computer vision, and real-time decision-making instead of the rigid, button-driven interfaces most autonomous systems still rely on.

Why It Matters

Autonomy is a solved-enough engineering problem. Trust and usability aren't.

The gap holding back consumer confidence in autonomous and semi-autonomous vehicles is less about perception accuracy and more about whether a human can understand, question, and direct the system in the moment — the human-machine interface problem ChatPilot was built to explore.

Technical Depth

A full perception-to-action pipeline, not a chatbot bolted onto a car

ChatPilot's engineering is the most demanding of the project portfolio: five distinct AI subsystems, coordinated in real time, tested in the CARLA autonomous-driving simulator.

Capabilities

What the system actually does

Natural-language vehicle commands

Riders direct the vehicle conversationally instead of through fixed button presses or rigid command syntax.

Real-time obstacle detection

Computer-vision perception feeds directly into the planning layer for continuous situational awareness.

Adaptive route planning

A* pathfinding combined with reinforcement learning for decisions in dynamic, changing environments.

Multi-modal feedback loop

Voice and visual interface responses keep the rider informed of what the system is doing and why.

Business Relevance

Where a system like this creates commercial value

ChatPilot is a research and simulation build, not a deployed product — but the underlying interface pattern maps onto three real, growing markets.

In-Cabin Experience

Automaker differentiation

Conversational control is becoming a brand differentiator for automakers (as reflected in the growing automotive voice-recognition market above) — a natural extension point for this architecture.

Accessibility

Inclusive mobility

Natural-language vehicle interaction lowers the barrier for riders with limited mobility or dexterity, a use case regulators and OEMs increasingly prioritize.

Low-Speed Autonomy

Campus & warehouse robotics

Delivery robots and shuttles operating in constrained environments benefit from the same voice-override and obstacle-avoidance pattern ChatPilot demonstrates.

Positioning

Command-line autonomy vs. conversational autonomy

The comparison that motivated the project: most simulated/prototype autonomous systems in academic settings still rely on rigid, rule-based command interfaces.
